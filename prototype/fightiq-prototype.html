What it is

You leave the gym with three corrections in your head. By Thursday you remember one of them, and it's usually not the important one.

Notes apps don't fix that. They give you a wall of text you never reread, and no one is reading it back to you. FightIQ is the part that reads it back: it takes a session note written the way you'd tell a training partner about it, and returns what you learned, what gave you trouble, why that thing keeps happening, and one drill for next session.

It keeps what it has seen. Log the same stall twice and it stops being a bad round — it becomes the thing at the top of your home screen, with the receipts for why it's there.

The loop

STEP 01

Log

Type it or talk it, in your own words. No forms, no dropdowns, no tagging your own training.

STEP 02

Read

A session analysis in the language your coach used, ending in one specific thing to drill.

STEP 03

See it

Can't picture the correction? FightIQ animates the movement step by step, then links the footage worth watching.

Seeing the technique

"Turn the corner with your hips" is a sentence. It isn't a picture, and a sentence is a bad way to learn a movement you've never felt. So a correction doesn't stop at text — it opens into a motion breakdown you can pause and step through inside the app, and a shortlist of real footage on YouTube where someone does it properly at full speed.

The two do different jobs. The breakdown is slow, labelled and specific to your mistake. The footage shows you the real thing, with timing and resistance the diagram can't fake.

Before you tap

What's real in this demo

  • The analysis is pre-written. Your note is matched to one of five prepared reads by keyword — there's no model running on this page.
  • The motion breakdowns are hand-drawn schematics for four techniques. They animate real coaching points; they are not footage of a real athlete.
  • The YouTube links are real and open in a new tab. They're the same vetted studies the production app ships with.
  • Nothing is saved. Reload and the demo starts clean.
  • Performance detail, settings and anything with a price on it are stubs — they'll tell you so when you tap them.
